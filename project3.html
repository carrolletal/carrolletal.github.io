Forebrain circuits for sensory representations

Sensory perception arises in cortex, but it is a systemic process that depends on cortical interconnections with thalamus and extrathalamic subcortical structures [1-2]. Forebrain circuits exert top-down control through these descending cortical projections, and the increasingly complex sensory representations that emerge across the cortical hierarchy are shaped by feedback as well as feedforward connectivity. We study the forebrain representations that support this control and how they shape information processing in hierarchically distinct targets, combining neuronal recordings, optogenetic perturbations and behavioral paradigms to discern how these projections contribute to visually guided behavior.

Visual cortical activity during passive viewing

In collaboration with the Sherman lab at The University of Chicago, we study transthalamic feedback projections from primary visual cortex (V1) to the higher-order thalamic nucleus pulvinar. We perturb this pathway with optogenetics while recording visually evoked single-unit responses in V1 using Neuropixels probes. Our preliminary evidence suggests this feedback pathway enhances V1 tuning, consistent with a modulatory function.

Psychometrics for circuit dissection

To link these circuits to perception, we train mice on simple visual tasks, then briefly switch specific pathways on or off with light to see how perception changes. We are piloting the MoReBees system in collaboration with the Cone Lab at the University of Calgary. Understanding these circuits matters beyond basic science, because breakdowns in communication between thalamus and cortex are linked to schizophrenia, autism, epilepsy and attention disorders.
